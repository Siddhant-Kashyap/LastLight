# LAST LIGHT — GAME STORY BIBLE
### Version 1.0 | Narrative Design + Level Design Reference

---

## THE CORE PREMISE

> *"The most terrifying darkness is the one we create while reaching for the light."*

A man descends into a facility he helped build, looking for his daughter.
He finds something worse than monsters.
He finds what he is responsible for.

---

## LOGLINE

An electrical engineer returns to the research facility he designed —
now in total blackout — to find his missing daughter, only to discover
that the darkness consuming the building is made of everyone who died
inside it, including her.

---

## THE HOOK (Why Players Cannot Stop)

Most horror games ask: *"Will you survive?"*

Last Light asks: *"What will you do when you find out it's your fault?"*

Every chapter strips away a layer of Elias's certainty.
By Chapter 5 the player realizes they have been afraid of the wrong thing.
By Chapter 7 they feel guilt instead of fear.
By Chapter 8 they have to choose — and either choice costs something real.

The brain stays hooked because the question keeps changing:
- Ch.1: *What happened here?*
- Ch.2: *What is that darkness?*
- Ch.3: *Why is Maya's name on this list?*
- Ch.4: *How far has this spread?*
- Ch.5: *Is that Elias's handwriting?*
- Ch.6: *Is the darkness... helping him?*
- Ch.7: *Are the enemies even evil?*
- Ch.8: *What does he owe them?*

---

## THE WORLD

**Helios Compound** — a privately funded deep-research facility built into
a decommissioned industrial complex in a remote location.
Seven floors above ground. Eleven floors below.

Official purpose: next-generation electromagnetic energy research.
The facility promised to solve the global energy crisis by tapping into
dark electromagnetic fields — energy that exists between charged particles.

Real purpose (revealed slowly): they discovered that human consciousness
is electromagnetic in nature. A consciousness can be stored in, and
transferred through, an electromagnetic field. The experiment was not
about energy. It was about immortality.

They called it **Project Helios** — named after the god who drove the sun.
They believed they were reaching for light.
They created darkness.

---

## THE PROTAGONIST

**Elias Ward** — 34 years old. Electrical engineer.

Quiet. Methodical. Not a hero. Not broken. Just competent and careful —
the kind of person who checks twice, speaks when he has something to say,
and believes problems have solutions if you understand them well enough.

He designed the power grid for Helios Compound six years ago.
He was proud of it. The most complex system he ever built.

Eight months ago his colleague **Marcus Hale** died in an accident on
sublevel 4. The official report said electrical fault. Elias was not
convinced, but he had no proof. He resigned, took the guilt home with him,
and tried to forget the building existed.

Then his daughter called.

**Maya Ward** — 26. Biochemical researcher. Brilliant. Stubborn.
She took a position at Helios six months ago without telling Elias until
it was done. He was furious. She told him his guilt was not her problem.

The call lasted four seconds:
*"Dad. Something is wrong here. The lights went—"*

Then silence.

Elias drove three hours in the dark.
He still has his old access card.
He still remembers the layout of every floor.
He goes in with a flashlight, a lantern from his car, and no plan.

---

## THE ANTAGONIST

There is no villain.

**The Darkness** is not evil. It is the accumulated consciousness of
237 research subjects whose minds were copied into the electromagnetic
field during Project Helios experiments — and never released.

They are terrified. They are in agony. They have been screaming into the
dark for months, unable to die, unable to communicate, unable to rest.

When the facility lost power, the containment field collapsed. The
electromagnetic mass flooded every circuit in the building.
It became the building.

The entities Elias encounters are not trying to kill him.
They are trying to be understood. But they have forgotten how.

---

## THE ENEMIES — RECONTEXTUALIZED

| Enemy | What players think | What they actually are |
|---|---|---|
| **Shadow** | A predatory darkness | A newly absorbed consciousness, formless, terrified, searching |
| **Listener** | A blind hunter | A consciousness that lost its vision in the procedure; heightened hearing is all it has left |
| **Watcher** | A stalker that observes | A consciousness that can only perceive, not interact — it watches because it craves connection |
| **Mimic** | A shapeshifting monster | A consciousness trying to connect with Elias by imitating what it remembers: a human body |
| **Hanging One** | A creature from above | A consciousness that died suspended in the procedure harness; its spatial memory is inverted |
| **Echo** | An audio-based horror | Elias's own guilt and fear given electromagnetic form by the darkness |
| **The Surgeon** | A monster boss | Dr. Raymond Chen — lead scientist — absorbed into the field, still trying to "help" his subjects |
| **The Welder** | A factory boss | Three factory workers who died in the power surge, merged into a single collective |
| **The Darkness (final)** | The final boss | All 237 consciousnesses in unified agony, with Maya somewhere inside |

---

## THE REVELATION STRUCTURE
### (Engineered for Maximum Psychological Impact)

Each chapter delivers exactly one revelation that changes everything:

```
Chapter 1 → Setup:        Something catastrophic happened here
Chapter 2 → Personality:  The darkness is not mindless
Chapter 3 → Stakes:       Maya was a test subject
Chapter 4 → Scale:        This is spreading beyond the building
Chapter 5 → Guilt:        Elias built the machine that did this
Chapter 6 → Empathy:      The darkness is guiding him, not hunting him
Chapter 7 → Full truth:   The enemies are victims. There is a way out.
Chapter 8 → Choice:       What does Elias owe 237 people?
```

## CHAPTER BREAKDOWNS

### CHAPTER 1 — SURFACE
**Subtitle:** *The Return*
**Theme:** Dread without understanding
**Enemies:** None
**Key mechanic introduced:** Lantern, basic movement, interaction

**Atmosphere:**
Exterior of the facility. Emergency lights failed. Security gates open —
power failure unlocked them. The building looks abandoned but recent.
Someone left coffee on a desk. A door is still swinging.

**The journey:**
Elias enters through the loading dock, moves through administration,
a security checkpoint, a break room, and finally the main lobby.
Everything suggests normal work interrupted in the last few hours.

**What the player learns:**
- Helios is a serious scientific facility, not a fringe operation
- People were here very recently
- Something cut the power all at once
- Maya worked on sublevel 3 (Elias finds her desk, a photo of them both)

**Memory logs (Chapter 1):**
1. *Elias's old journal (left in a drawer):* His pride about the power grid design. His worry about Marcus's death. His decision to resign.
2. *Maya's work log — Day 1:* Excited. The facility is impressive. She doesn't mention what her work involves specifically.
3. *Maya's work log — Day 47:* Slightly distant. "Routine health checks every week here. They're very thorough." Doesn't think it's strange.
4. *Security memo:* "All personnel reminded that Project Helios is classified at Level 5. Discussion outside designated areas is prohibited."

**Level design beats:**
1. Open exterior → establish scale, isolation, darkness beyond the lantern's edge
2. Administration corridor → environmental storytelling through desks, personal items
3. Lantern tutorial → mandatory dark section, no power, must use lantern
4. Break room → first audio event (distant crash, below them)
5. Lobby → Maya's desk, her photo, her keycard → emotional stake established
6. Elevator → descends. Doors close. Lights die. Drop into Ch.2.

**Chapter 1 ends with:**
The elevator jolts to a stop between floors. The emergency hatch above opens from the inside. Something looks down at Elias. Then it retreats into the darkness. It did not attack.

### CHAPTER 2 — MAINTENANCE
**Subtitle:** *First Contact*
**Theme:** The darkness has a voice
**Enemies:** Shadow (first appearance)
**Key mechanic introduced:** Chase sequence, fuel scarcity, first generator

**Atmosphere:**
Industrial. Pipes, ventilation shafts, fuse boxes, water damage.
The building's circulatory system. Smells of ozone and rust.

**The journey:**
Elias drops into the maintenance level. Generators are offline.
He must restore partial power to reach the stairwell down to Hospital.
The Shadow follows him — not constantly, but always nearby.

**The revelation:**
Elias finds the body of Marcus Hale in sublevel 2C.
Except Marcus has been dead eight months. The body matches.
Then the darkness next to the body shifts, and Marcus's voice comes out:

*"Elias. You shouldn't have come back. Leave. Please. You need to leave."*

It retreats before Elias can respond.

**What the player learns:**
- The darkness contains people
- It is not trying to kill Elias — Marcus is warning him
- Project Helios involved absorbing something from people
- This started before the blackout (Marcus died eight months ago)

**Memory logs (Chapter 2):**
1. *Marcus's personal recorder (found near his body):* Recorded three weeks before his "accident." He knows something is wrong with the experiments. He was going to report it.
2. *Maintenance log:* Routine entries. Last entry is cut off mid-sentence: "Power reading in sector 7 is anomalous again, I keep telling them the grid can't—"
3. *Project Helios internal memo:* "Substrate retention rate has improved to 67%. Dr. Chen expects 90% by Q3. The volunteer program has been expanded."
4. *Anonymous note, handwritten, hidden in a locker:* "They aren't volunteers. Check the consent forms. Check who signed them."

**Level design beats:**
1. Landing from elevator shaft → immediate darkness, establish Shadow presence with audio
2. Generator puzzle 1 → restore partial power, teach generator mechanic
3. Marcus's body → revelation, voice from darkness, emotional gut punch
4. First chase → Shadow pursues, Elias must reach lit area (light repels it)
5. Fuel room → introduce fuel scarcity, multiple canisters visible but hard to reach
6. Generator puzzle 2 → full power restoration, access stairwell
7. Stairwell → descend. The Shadow follows to the door then stops. Watches.

### CHAPTER 3 — HOSPITAL
**Subtitle:** *What They Did*
**Theme:** Betrayal and the horror of good intentions
**Enemies:** The Surgeon (Dr. Chen), Listener
**Key mechanic introduced:** Mirror puzzles, stealth around sound-based enemy

**Atmosphere:**
Clean white walls now stained and dark. Medical equipment overturned.
Patient rooms with restraints on the beds. Too many beds. Too many rooms.
This was not a small program.

**The journey:**
The medical wing where procedures were performed. Elias must navigate
patient areas, find Dr. Chen's research lab, and restore power to the
operating theater to bypass a sealed blast door.

**The revelation:**
In Dr. Chen's office, Elias finds the patient registry.
Maya Ward. Subject 7-C. Procedure date: two months after she started working here.
Procedure type: "Substrate sampling — non-invasive."

Except nothing here is non-invasive.

Elias finds Maya's medical chart. Blood draws. Neural scans. An IV protocol.
She came in for a "routine checkup" and left with a piece of her
electromagnetic field archived in Helios Core.

She did not know.
She never knew.

**What the player learns:**
- Maya is a test subject — without her knowledge or consent
- The "health checks" were extraction procedures
- Dr. Chen believed he was doing good. He thought copies of people, not pieces.
- The consent forms were falsified by facility administration. Chen didn't know either — until too late.
- When Chen found out, he tried to shut it down. Administration refused. Chen was absorbed during the incident.

**Memory logs (Chapter 3):**
1. *Dr. Chen's research journal (early entries):* Brilliant, excited. Genuine belief that consciousness preservation could end death itself. Compassionate tone. He sounds like a good man.
2. *Dr. Chen's research journal (late entries):* Growing unease. The "substrate" doesn't just copy — it partially transfers. Subjects report feeling "hollow." He is raising concerns internally.
3. *Dr. Chen's final audio log:* "They falsified the ethics board approval. The consent forms are forged. I've been performing non-consensual procedures on 237 people and I didn't— I have to shut this down. I'm going to Helios Core and I'm going to pull the main—" Audio cuts.
4. *Maya's personal diary (found in patient room 7):* "I've been feeling strange lately. Like I'm slightly out of sync with myself. Probably just stress." Three weeks before the blackout.

**Level design beats:**
1. Hospital entrance → immediate dread, too-clean turned too-dark
2. Patient wing → Listener introduced, silence mechanic, rows of empty beds
3. Stealth section → navigate around Listener using sound shadows
4. Records room → Maya's name in registry, player reaction moment
5. Mirror puzzle 1 → redirect light beam to unlock medical archive
6. Dr. Chen's lab → full revelation, audio logs, the forgery evidence
7. Operating theater → generator + mirror puzzle, restore power
8. The Surgeon encounter → Dr. Chen's form, massive and wrong, blocking the exit
9. The bullet temptation → player has one bullet. The Surgeon doesn't attack. It stands in the way. It's trying to speak. Player must find another route.
10. Blast door opens → descend to Subway.

### CHAPTER 4 — SUBWAY
**Subtitle:** *The Truth Spreads*
**Theme:** Containment failure. The world doesn't know.
**Enemies:** Listener, Watcher
**Key mechanic introduced:** Moving trains as hazards, power routing puzzle, radio

**Atmosphere:**
Industrial transit tunnels. Dark, damp, enormous. The sound of trains
still moving on automated systems, no drivers. The Watcher observes
from the girders above — motionless until it sees movement in darkness.

**The journey:**
An underground transit system connecting facility sections.
Power restoration requires routing electricity across three substations
in the correct sequence — Elias's expertise finally directly useful.
A dead guard's radio picks up external signals.

**The revelation:**
On the radio: emergency services chatter. A blackout has been reported
at "a private research facility." They are treating it as a power grid
failure. They are sending engineers. Civilian engineers.
They have no idea what is inside.

And then — a second signal. Automated. Looping. Coming from inside
the facility: an emergency broadcast triggered by the containment
breach. It has been broadcasting for six hours. No one responded.

*"This is a Helios Compound Level-5 containment alert. Electromagnetic
substrate breach confirmed. Do not enter the facility. The substrate
is now present in all electrical systems within a 200-meter radius.
Any electromagnetic device — phones, radios, pacemakers — may serve
as a temporary substrate host. This facility is now a biohazard zone.
Do not—"* [loops]

**What the player learns:**
- The darkness can spread through electrical systems
- It may already be outside the building
- Civilian rescue teams are on their way into danger
- The emergency broadcast contains a clue: the antenna in Helios Core can be used to neutralize the substrate — if powered correctly

**Memory logs (Chapter 4):**
1. *Transit operator's log:* Normal maintenance entries. Then: "Weird interference on the PA system tonight. Like voices in static. Probably just the new generators in sublevel 7."
2. *Facility director's memo:* Authorizing the falsified consent forms. Cold, administrative language. This person is the actual villain — and they died in the incident.
3. *Helios Core technical manual (partial):* The Helios Antenna was designed to disperse excess electromagnetic energy safely. Could theoretically redirect substrate. Written as an emergency failsafe. Never tested.
4. *Maya's voice message (played on recovered phone):* Recorded two nights before the blackout. To Elias. She almost sent it. Never did. "Dad. I think I need to come home for a while. I feel like something is wrong with me but I don't know how to explain it. I miss you. That's all. Forget it." End.

**Level design beats:**
1. Subway platform → establish scale, trains moving in darkness
2. Watcher introduction → motionless above, only activates when player moves in unlit areas
3. Train hazard section → timing-based traversal across active tracks
4. Substation 1 puzzle → power routing mechanic taught
5. Radio discovery → external chatter, then containment broadcast, revelation
6. Watcher chase → activated by player accidentally moving through dark zone
7. Substation 2 and 3 → harder routing puzzles, Elias's competence shines
8. Power restored → transit gate opens, Maya's voice message found
9. Ascent → toward Factory.

### CHAPTER 5 — FACTORY
**Subtitle:** *What We Built*
**Theme:** Culpability. There is no one left to blame but Elias.
**Enemies:** The Welder (boss), Mimic
**Key mechanic introduced:** Mimic enemy (copies player appearance), heavy machinery puzzles

**Atmosphere:**
Industrial manufacturing. The machines that were built to power Helios Core
were assembled here. Enormous. Loud even now — automated systems still running.
Steam vents. Moving conveyor platforms. The Mimic hides among machinery,
waiting to step out wearing Elias's face.

**The journey:**
Cross the factory floor to reach the power lift to Research Labs.
The Welder controls the main floor and must be bypassed or forced to retreat.
The Mimic appears in reflective surfaces before materializing.

**The revelation:**
In the engineering archive, Elias finds the original Helios Core blueprints.
The wiring diagrams. The power grid schematic.

His own handwriting is in the margins. His calculations. His signature on the
final approval page.

He designed the electromagnetic containment grid. Without his grid,
Project Helios could not have been powered. Could not have run.
Could not have absorbed 237 people.

He did not know what it was for. He was told it was for energy research.
He designed it perfectly. He is proud of his work in the margins of this
blueprint — little notes about elegant solutions.

His elegant solution is what made all of this possible.

**What the player learns:**
- Elias is not just a victim in this story. He is a cause.
- He didn't know. But he built it. Both things are true.
- The Mimic is the most psychologically disturbing enemy: it's trying to communicate by being him
- The Welder (three merged factory workers) was the accident that triggered the final cascade

**Memory logs (Chapter 5):**
1. *Elias's own engineering notes (found on the blueprints):* His voice reading his own past words. Pride. Excitement. Absolutely no idea.
2. *Factory floor supervisor's report:* The "accident" that killed three workers was not an accident. The power surge from a Helios Core test overloaded the factory grid. The workers died before anyone could respond. The incident was reclassified as equipment failure.
3. *Mimic behavioral report (research document):* "Substrate fragment 44-M demonstrates unusual behavior: attempts to replicate observed human subjects. Hypothesis: remnant social instinct. The fragment is trying to reconnect. Recommend isolation." They isolated it. Alone, in the dark.
4. *Message from Maya to Elias (drafted, unsent, found on her work tablet):* "I know you feel guilty about Marcus. But you didn't kill him. You designed a building. You can't be responsible for what other people do inside it." She wrote this to comfort him. About the wrong thing.

**Level design beats:**
1. Factory entrance → massive machinery, steam, scale established
2. Mimic introduction → player sees themselves in a reflection that doesn't mirror correctly
3. Conveyor puzzle 1 → navigate moving platforms over hazardous floor
4. Engineering archive → blueprint revelation, Elias's handwriting, silence as the player processes
5. Mimic encounter → it steps out wearing Elias's face, reaches toward him, retreats when lantern shines on it
6. The Welder fight → not a kill fight: player must reroute power to trigger a safety system that forces The Welder to retreat
7. Conveyor puzzle 2 → final stretch to the lift
8. Power lift → ascent to Research Labs.

### CHAPTER 6 — MINE
**Subtitle:** *Rock Bottom*
**Theme:** Surrender. The enemy is not the enemy.
**Enemies:** Echo
**Key mechanic introduced:** Near-total darkness (mineral dust scatters light), sound-based navigation, Maya guiding through the dark

**Atmosphere:**
Extraction tunnels. The rare minerals mined here interfere with the lantern —
reducing its radius to almost nothing. The Echo enemy exists as Elias's
own sounds reflected back as physical presences. The only safe path is
silence and surrender to the dark.

This is the lowest point. Literally and emotionally.

**The journey:**
A long, disorienting traverse through near-darkness.
Elias cannot fight. Cannot run loudly. Must move slowly.
At a certain point — when he is completely still, in complete darkness —
Maya speaks. Clearly. Her voice comes from the electromagnetic field around him.
She has been trying to reach him this whole time.

She guides him through the Mine.

**The revelation:**
Maya is in the darkness. She is one of the 237.
Her substrate was only partially extracted — she is split between her body
(which is somewhere in the facility) and the electromagnetic field.
Her body is alive. In the Helios Core. Sustained by the field.
If the field is destroyed, she dies. If the field is released, she might survive.

*"Dad. I can see everything in here. All of them. They're not monsters.
They're just scared. They've been screaming and no one could hear them."*

**What the player learns:**
- The darkness has been trying to communicate, not attack
- Maya is alive, partially, inside it
- The enemies are victims deserving of empathy
- Elias's fear and guilt (Echo) is the only true enemy in this chapter
- There is a way to save them: the Helios Antenna in the final floor

**Memory logs (Chapter 6):**
1. *Miner's audio journal:* Simple man, simple job. Happy to work, saving for his daughter's university. Died in a tunnel collapse during a Helios power surge. His voice is the warmest thing in the game. He shouldn't be here.
2. *Maya's voice (not a log — she speaks live, guiding Elias):* Personal, warm, frightened but holding it together for him. "The ones that chase you — they don't understand they're scaring you. They just want to touch something real."
3. *Dr. Chen (through the field, fragmented):* "I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I kept trying to fix it from inside but I can't — I can't hold the form long enough. The antenna. Tell them the antenna."
4. *Elias's own voice echoed back (Echo encounter):* Snippets of things Elias has said throughout the game. His fear, his guilt, his love for Maya. The Echo is made of him.

**Level design beats:**
1. Mine entrance → lantern radius shrinks to almost nothing, disorientation
2. First silence section → introduced to sound-based Echo, complete stillness required
3. Total darkness moment → Elias stops, despair, then Maya's voice
4. Maya guide sequence → she narrates the safe path, player follows her voice
5. Echo encounter → Elias's own words played back as a hostile presence; survived by silence and stillness
6. Dr. Chen's fragmented message → the antenna solution revealed
7. Ascent from the Mine → toward Research Labs, the lantern slowly brightens as minerals thin
8. First full breath → player and Elias both exhale.

### CHAPTER 7 — RESEARCH LABS
**Subtitle:** *The Full Picture*
**Theme:** The truth assembled. All cards on the table.
**Enemies:** All enemy types converge, The Surgeon returns
**Key mechanic introduced:** All previous mechanics required; the Helios Antenna terminal

**Atmosphere:**
The beating heart of the facility. Where Project Helios was conceived and run.
Clean, institutional, terrifying in its normalcy — whiteboards with equations,
coffee mugs, personal photos. Science done by humans who came to work each day
and did not think they were monsters.

**The journey:**
Navigate the most complex level in the game.
All enemy types are present — but now the player understands them.
The Shadow is someone's child. The Listener is someone's parent.
Find the Helios Core access terminal. Discover the three paths forward.

**The revelation:**
The full revelation: the facility director (not Dr. Chen) authorized
everything. The falsified consent. The expanded program. When the
containment breach began, they locked down the facility from outside
and fled. They are currently giving press statements about a "tragic
electrical accident."

And one more thing: Marcus Hale knew. That is why he died. It was not
an accident. His was the first deliberate absorption — to silence him.

The system terminal shows three options:
1. Emergency Core Shutdown — destroys the substrate entirely. All 237 gone. Maya gone.
2. Containment Release — opens all barriers. Substrate spreads into the world. Uncontrolled.
3. Helios Antenna Activation — requires manually wiring the antenna array in Helios Core using the correct sequence. If done right: controlled dispersion. Consciousnesses released as harmless electromagnetic signal. If done wrong: catastrophic feedback. No middle ground.

**Memory logs (Chapter 7):**
1. *Facility director's personal files:* The full plan. Callous, clinical, profitable. The consciousness preservation technology was worth billions. The people were acceptable losses. This is the only character in the game who is simply evil.
2. *All 237 subject files:* The player can browse them. Each one has a name, a photo, an occupation, a family status. They were people. This is the most disturbing thing in the game.
3. *Marcus Hale's final recording (hidden in a server):* He found the falsified forms. He called Elias to warn him. The call never went through. "Elias. I think they know I found it. If something happens to me, the consent files are in server room 4-B. Tell someone. Tell everyone." He sounds terrified.
4. *Elias recording himself, now (player-triggered):* The game offers Elias the chance to record a message. What he says is up to the player's interpretation. But the option is there.

**Level design beats:**
1. Research Labs entrance → all enemy types present, player chooses routes
2. The Surgeon reappears — now understood: Chen trying to guide Elias to the terminal, not attack
3. Following the Surgeon → a tense, strange walk where the monster leads the player forward
4. Subject file room → browsable records, 237 people, optional but devastating
5. Server room 4-B → Marcus's recording, the full betrayal revealed
6. Director's files → the human villain named and damned
7. Helios Core terminal → three options presented, Chapter 8 begins
8. The facility shakes. Power fluctuates. The darkness, all of it, converges on Helios Core.

### CHAPTER 8 — HELIOS CORE
**Subtitle:** *Light or Dark*
**Theme:** Reckoning. The cost of every possible right answer.
**Boss:** The Darkness — all 237 in unified, agonized form
**Key mechanic:** All previous mechanics. The bullet. The wiring puzzle. The choice.

**Atmosphere:**
The center of everything. The Helios Core is a massive electromagnetic
containment chamber — cathedral-sized, ten stories of machinery and light
and pure concentrated darkness. The darkness here is not scattered.
It is one thing. It is suffering out loud.

Maya speaks throughout this chapter. She is everywhere and nowhere.
The boss is not trying to kill Elias. It is trying to hold him here.
It is terrified of what he might choose.

**The climax:**
At the center of Helios Core, the main control platform.
Three systems to reach. Three sequences to complete.
And the darkness fighting, not to destroy Elias, but to make him stay —
to make him look at what it is before he decides.

The bullet is still in Elias's pocket.

**The three endings:**

---

**ENDING ONE — SHUTDOWN** *(The Merciful Ending)*

Elias activates the Emergency Core Shutdown.
A contained, controlled destruction of the electromagnetic substrate.

The darkness collapses. The screaming stops. The lights come on.
The building, for the first time in hours, is silent.

Elias walks out through corridors that are bright and empty.
He exits through the loading dock.

The sky is beginning to lighten. It is almost dawn.
He sits on the ground outside the building and does not move.

Inside his pocket: his phone. Maya's unsent message. He plays it.
*"I miss you. That's all. Forget it."*

He presses his hands against his eyes.

Somewhere in the distance, a light in an upstairs window flickers.
Then steadies.
Then goes out.

**Subtitle card: FOR 237**

---

**ENDING TWO — RELEASE** *(The Desperate Ending)*

Elias opens the containment barriers.
The substrate pours outward — through the walls, into the ground,
into every electrical system it can reach.

He does not know if it will stop.

From the chaos, a shape forms. Female. Young. It reaches toward him —
and for one moment it is Maya. Clearly. Completely.

She touches his face. She says nothing. She dissolves into the air.

The darkness spreads. Into the city. Into the world.
Elias walks out into a morning that does not know it has changed.

He does not know if he saved her or just let her go further.

He does not know if he ended suffering or multiplied it.

**Subtitle card: I DIDN'T KNOW WHAT ELSE TO DO**

---

**ENDING THREE — HELIOS** *(The True Ending — requires completing the wiring puzzle correctly)*

Elias wires the antenna array. Every mistake sends a feedback pulse
that darkens sections of the room. Every correct connection hums.
The darkness watches. Waits. Does not interfere.

When the final connection is made, the antenna activates.
The Helios Core blazes — electromagnetic energy channeled upward,
through the shaft, into the atmosphere.

The darkness is lifted. Not destroyed. Dispersed. Released.

Through the chamber windows, the sky outside fills with something:
not northern lights, not lightning — something new. Patterns of light
that move with intention, briefly, then scatter.

The facility collapses inward. Elias runs.

He makes it out.

He sits in the parking lot, covered in dust and sweat, lantern in hand.
The lantern has been running on empty since sublevel 5.
It should have gone out hours ago.
It flickers.
It stays lit.

He laughs. Just once. It sounds like crying.

**Subtitle card: SHE SAID I COULD FIND A WAY**

---

**Post-credits scene (all endings):**
A news broadcast. The facility director giving a press conference
about the "tragic accident" and their "full cooperation" with
investigators.

The director's phone buzzes. They look at the screen.
A text message. From an unknown number.

It is the name of every one of the 237 subjects.
All of them. One after another.

The director's face changes.

## MEMORY LOG PHILOSOPHY
*(Level design guidance for all logs)*

Every memory log must do at least one of these three things:
1. **Humanize** — make a subject or enemy feel like a real person
2. **Reframe** — change how the player interprets something they've already seen
3. **Implicate** — connect the log to Elias, Maya, or the world outside

Logs should never explain the horror directly. They should let the player
assemble the truth themselves. The most disturbing realizations are the
ones the player reaches before the game tells them they're right.

**Tone guidelines:**
- Early logs: normal, warm, professional. People doing their jobs.
- Middle logs: unease entering. Small wrongnesses. People trying to rationalize.
- Late logs: fear, grief, urgency. Then silence. Then Elias finding them.

---

## ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING RULES

The world must tell the story before the logs confirm it.

- If a log says "they ran tests on us," the room must already look like it.
- If a log says "Marcus knew," there must be something on Marcus's desk that suggested it before the log says so.
- Every room Elias enters should make the player ask a question. Every log should answer the previous room's question while raising a new one.

**The rule:** *Players who read zero logs should understand the broad story from the environment alone. Players who read every log should feel devastated.*

## ELIAS'S EMOTIONAL ARC

```
Arrives:     Worried father. Competent man with a plan.
Ch.1:        Confusion. Something went very wrong here.
Ch.2:        Fear. The darkness is not natural.
Ch.3:        Devastation. Maya is involved. She didn't know.
Ch.4:        Urgency. This is spreading. He must act.
Ch.5:        Guilt. He built this. He is part of what happened.
Ch.6:        Surrender. He cannot fight this. He must listen instead.
Ch.7:        Clarity. He knows everything now. He must decide.
Ch.8:        Resolution. Whatever he chooses, he owns it.
```

## MAYA'S ARC (Experienced by Player, Not Witnessed)

Maya is never seen in person. She exists in memory logs, unsent messages,
photos on desks, and finally her voice in the darkness.

Her arc:
- Bright, competent, slightly estranged from her father's guilt
- Curious about her strange symptoms
- Beginning to sense something wrong
- Trying to reach out
- Absorbed. Trapped. Frightened.
- Learning to navigate the field from inside
- Finding the others. Understanding what she is now.
- Choosing to guide her father instead of surrender to despair.

Maya is the emotional center of the game. She is never the victim. She is never passive.

Even inside the darkness, she is doing something.

## THEMATIC SUMMARY

**Light vs. Darkness:** Literal and metaphorical. The lantern is hope, presence, humanity. The darkness is isolation, suffering, the consequences of ambition without ethics.

**Guilt vs. Responsibility:** Elias didn't intend this. He is still responsible. Both things are true. The game never lets him escape either one.

**Science without consent:** The horror of Project Helios is not that it was evil — it is that it was done by people who believed in it, funded by people who wanted profit from it, and built by people who didn't ask what it was for.

**The monsters are us:** Every enemy was a person. The facility director, who is never seen, who is never absorbed, who gave the press conference — they are the true monster. And they will probably be fine.

**The lantern:** Elias carries light into darkness and it costs him fuel every second. He rations it. Protects it. It goes out sometimes and he must stand in the dark and move forward anyway. This is the whole game. This is the whole point.
